A locational astrology study tool

Astrocartography and location astrology, explained

Spectral Atlas is an interactive map for exploring how astrologers relate a birth chart to places around the world. Enter birth date, time, and location to calculate planetary angular lines, then inspect cities, crossings, parans, and relocation charts. This guide explains what the map represents and how to use its features.

What the tool calculates

At a selected moment, each planet occupies a position in the sky and may be rising, setting, at the Midheaven (culminating), or at the Imum Coeli (nadir) somewhere on Earth. Spectral Atlas calculates planetary positions and projects those angular relationships onto a world map. In In Mundo mode, lines represent physical angular positions; in In Zodiaco mode, the map relates planetary zodiacal longitude to relocated chart angles. The method changes the interpretation of a line, so compare modes rather than assuming that every line is equivalent.

The tool can also display planetary aspects, latitude-based parans, local-space bearings, line crossings, transit lines, and a relocated chart for a destination. These are analytical layers for study; a dense map is easier to read when you enable a small number of relevant layers at a time.

How to use Spectral Atlas

  1. Enter the birth date, exact local birth time, birthplace, and the correct time-zone offset. If the time is uncertain, treat angle-based lines and houses cautiously.
  2. Select a map mode and planet or angle layers. Use the search field to find a city, then inspect nearby lines and their angular labels.
  3. Open a line, crossing, or paran to compare its geometry and astrological interpretation. Distance from a line is a descriptive input, not a guarantee of an effect.
  4. Use relocation and transit views to compare a destination or date with the natal map. Save or export a report only after checking the underlying birth data.

Astrocartography glossary

Ascendant (ASC)
The eastern horizon point; an ASC line marks where a planet was rising at the selected moment.
Descendant (DSC)
The western horizon point; a DSC line marks where a planet was setting.
Midheaven (MC)
The upper meridian angle associated with culmination; an MC line marks where a planet was overhead.
Imum Coeli (IC)
The lower meridian angle opposite the Midheaven; an IC line marks where a planet was beneath the local meridian.
Paran
A latitude-based relationship where two celestial bodies are simultaneously angular; parans are usually interpreted by their latitude band.
Relocation chart
A natal chart recalculated for a different location while retaining the original birth moment. The planetary signs remain tied to that moment while angles and houses can change.
Transit
A comparison between planetary positions on a selected date and a natal chart. In this tool, transit lines are exploratory overlays rather than forecasts.

Interpretation, accuracy, and limitations

The calculations use the entered local birth time and time-zone offset to establish a UTC moment, then calculate planetary positions and project angular geometry. A mistaken time zone, daylight-saving adjustment, or imprecise birth time can shift chart angles and their mapped lines. Check source data and consult a qualified ephemeris if exact timing is important to your study.

Astrology is a symbolic tradition, not a scientifically validated predictive method. A map cannot determine whether a city is safe, affordable, healthy, or right for an individual. Use practical research and personal circumstances for real-world decisions; treat location scores and interpretive descriptions as prompts for reflection, not objective measurements.

Why the map is interactive

The application combines a React and TypeScript interface with astronomical calculations from Astronomy Engine, a 2D map layer, and a Three.js globe view. Dragging or zooming changes the visible geography; layer controls focus the map on selected planets and relationships. The globe is a navigational visualization of Earth and the calculated lines.

Frequently asked questions

What information do I need for an astrocartography map?

You need a birth date, birth time, and birthplace. The local time-zone offset is important because it determines the UTC moment used for planetary positions and chart angles.

What is the difference between a planet's MC and ASC lines?

An MC line follows locations where the planet culminates at the upper meridian; an ASC line follows locations where it rises at the eastern horizon. Astrologers assign different symbolic themes to these angles.

Do planetary lines guarantee an experience in a location?

No. Lines are calculated geometric relationships interpreted through astrology. They do not guarantee events or outcomes and should not replace practical decision-making.

Can I use the map if my birth time is approximate?

You can explore it, but rising and setting lines, house placements, and relocation angles are time-sensitive. Note the uncertainty and avoid treating fine geographic distinctions as precise.

What are parans and crossings?

Parans describe latitude bands where two bodies are simultaneously angular, while crossings mark intersections of mapped lines. Both are optional layers for comparing combined chart relationships.
